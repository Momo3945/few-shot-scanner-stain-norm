\subsection{The ablation ladder}
No rung comes close to the classical baselines on structure (SSIM 0.29--0.40 against at least 0.63) or on
A06 (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}), although on typical slides every rung's colour beats Reinhard and histogram matching. The colour LoRA (A2) recovers only
$\dlab=+2.25$ pooled, and on typical slides its LAB distance falls from 25.41 to 22.96. ControlNet raises
SSIM by about 0.10 (A0 0.285 to A1 0.389) but does not move colour. LCM-LoRA (A4) and the histopathology
warm-start (A5) cancel the small colour gain. Ranks 4 and 8 differ by only 0.1--0.3 in LAB distance, the size of run-to-run noise, so rank makes no measurable difference.
Sweeps show why denoising strength is a poor control knob: with 8 LCM steps, strengths 0.50 and 0.60 give
byte-identical outputs, because the number of steps actually run is $\lfloor 8s\rfloor$. Larger strengths
help the outlier slide A06 (the warm-started rung reaches $\dlab=+24.4$ at 0.70) but damage typical slides
(slide A08 $-7.0$), so no
single strength is usable.

\subsection{Source conditioning and DDIM inversion}
Conditioning on the source image gives a real but modest gain over the ladder ($\dlab=+3.41$ against $+2.25$ for A2; SSIM 0.448).
Replacing random img2img noise with DDIM inversion is the largest single improvement: LAB distance falls to
25.10 pooled and 17.98 on typical slides, every held-out slide moves towards the target (A06 recovery
$+21.7$), and SSIM rises to 0.496. In the reverse direction (\HtoA) the same model fails
($\dlab=-15.6$, a magenta drift), so the gain is direction-dependent. Replacing the Canny half of the
condition with HoVer-Net nuclear outlines, the planned final structural signal, does not help: fused SSIM is
unchanged (0.729) and windowed LAB is 1.3 worse (Appendix~\ref{app:hovernet}).

\subsection{The VAE ceiling}
\label{sec:vae}
SSIM stays far below the raw scan's 0.732 for every diffusion output because the VAE alone cannot
reproduce its input. Self-reconstruction SSIM on the held-out Aperio crops is 0.576 for the stock decoder,
0.618 for \texttt{ft-mse} and 0.646 for our fine-tuned decoder (Fig.~\ref{fig:vaebb}); on Hamamatsu crops it
is 0.726, 0.757 and 0.779, and the fine-tune is highest on every slide (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-vae}). It improves
100\% of Aperio crops, but the limit is generalisation rather than the frozen latent: the same decoder
trained on 16 crops reconstructs those crops at SSIM 0.88. Fine-tuning the encoder as well, anchored so that the
trained components still apply, adds at most 0.004 and nothing after fusion (Appendix~\ref{app:fullvae}). Across backbones the ceiling tracks latent
capacity: SDXL's four-channel VAE reconstructs at only 0.492, while the 16-channel VAE shared by SD\,3.5 and
PixCell reaches 0.773 (eight A06 crops each, so these two are indicative only).

Decoding the DDIM-inversion latents with the better decoders raises translated SSIM from 0.496 to 0.557
(\texttt{ft-mse}) and 0.616 (fine-tuned), winning on every crop. The fine-tune costs colour, though:
typical-slide LAB distance rises from 16.86 to 17.95 and A06 worsens to 79.2. Visual inspection explains
part of the SSIM gain. On two inspected crops the fine-tuned decoder keeps only 21--30\% of the input's
fine-detail energy (Laplacian variance), against 52--59\% for \texttt{ft-mse}, and shifts the blue channel
by 3--5 levels. The real Hamamatsu scans are themselves soft, at 18--24\% of the Aperio source's detail,
so much of the translated SSIM gain comes from matching the target scanner's softness rather than from
recovering structure. On unseen CAMELYON17 centres it still beats \texttt{ft-mse} on SSIM but loses 1.5\,dB of
PSNR to a colour shift (Appendix~\ref{app:decoder-cam}).

\subsection{Detail fusion}
\textbf{Choosing the variant.} Table~\ref{tab:fusion} compares the three fusion rules and the F3
parameters on internal training-domain crops. These crops are coordinate-corresponding rather than
registered, so absolute SSIM is low and only comparisons within the table are meaningful. Every variant
raises SSIM over the unfused prediction. F1 is worse than F3 on both axes; pasting the prediction's
unblurred chroma onto the source luminance is the likely cause. F2 sits at other points of the trade-off:
more SSIM for much worse colour ($\alpha=0.5$), or the lowest LAB distance at lower SSIM ($\alpha=1$). F3
was chosen as the best balance, and on visual inspection it was indistinguishable from the source in
nuclear boundaries and chromatin, with no haloes, ringing or gamut clipping.

\textbf{What the sweep shows.} SSIM is flat across $\beta$, from $0.10$ to $1.00$, and nearly flat across
$\sigma$ once $\sigma\geq8$. This is the mechanism of \eqref{eq:f3} at work: the blurred residual
cannot reach fine structure at any strength, so the structural gain comes from starting at the source
pixels at all. Colour traces a U-shape in $\beta$ with its minimum at 0.5. Small $\beta$ leaves the source
colour almost unchanged, and large $\beta$ adds more of a \emph{smoothed approximation} of the prediction's
shift, which is worse than the shift itself.

\textbf{Held-out result.} Fusing only the learned colour change onto the raw source raises SSIM to 0.729 pooled
and 0.746 on typical slides, essentially the raw scan's 0.732 and 0.748 and above every classical baseline, while keeping most of the colour shift:
typical-slide LAB distance is 18.11 (raw 25.41) and windowed LAB 18.65, closer to the target than Macenko
(21.88), Reinhard (27.42) or histogram matching (31.90). Fig.~\ref{fig:f3} shows the process. A06 benefits
least: fusion gives back 10.5 of the inversion's colour gain there (LAB 73.1 to 83.6). Fusion also absorbs other structural
gains: a three-member self-ensemble raises unfused SSIM from 0.496 to 0.543 but adds nothing after fusion,
and the fine-tuned decoder adds nothing either while worsening fused
windowed LAB from 27.08 to 27.74. In the failed \HtoA{} direction, fusion applied unchanged raises SSIM from
0.548 to 0.730 and shrinks the colour loss from $\dlab=-15.6$ to $-5.3$, although colour is still worse
than doing nothing. It also transfers to SDXL with the same frozen parameters (Table~\ref{tab:sdxl}).

\subsection{Learned baselines}
Trained on the same 50 pairs, ParamNet and StainGAN outperform every diffusion configuration in
Table~\ref{tab:main} (Fig.~\ref{fig:compare}). On typical slides the raw colour gap is 25.41: ParamNet closes about two-thirds of it (to 8.16),
StainGAN almost half (13.86) and our fused pipeline under a third (18.11). StainGAN also has the highest
SSIM, above even the raw scan's. These are means over three training seeds, and both methods beat our fused
pipeline on SSIM and typical-slide colour on every seed. StainNet, a per-pixel 1$\times$1 network with 1{,}283 parameters, is worse than doing
nothing on every typical slide and seed. A 150{,}000-step retrain plateaued at the same training loss, and the
network can represent the identity, so neither under-training nor capacity explains it; its paired L1
objective on misaligned pairs is a possible cause. The ranking also holds in the \HtoA{} direction on every seed: on typical slides ParamNet
recovers $\dlab=+17.17$ and StainGAN $+10.58$ (three-seed means), while StainNet loses $-23.73$. Reinhard and histogram matching only
beat the raw input pooled because of A06: on typical \AtoH{} slides Reinhard ($-1.38$) and histogram
matching ($-5.92$) are slightly worse than doing nothing, and only Macenko gains ($+4.39$).

\subsection{Structural safety and unseen centres}
Two normalisers pass the Relative Dice gate (Table~\ref{tab:reldice}): ParamNet and our final pipeline
(0.994, 95\% CI $[0.992, 0.997]$, entirely above 0.95). Our interval excludes 1.0, so the loss of about 0.6\%
is small but real rather than chance; only ParamNet, whose interval contains 1.0, also meets the stricter condition of no
statistically significant degradation. The pass comes from fusion, not from the diffusion
output itself: the unfused inversion output fails at 0.893, and fusion raises Dice on 129 of the 130 images
(mean $+0.071$). This is expected from \eqref{eq:f3}, which keeps most of the source's high frequencies and adds
only a blurred colour change, so nucleus boundaries come from the source. The LCM ladder setting (rung A4)
loses 12.6\% of HoVer-Net's agreement with the ground truth, and StainGAN, despite its high SSIM, loses 32\%,
which is consistent with GANs altering nuclei. Cycle consistency fails: translating 64 A06 crops \AtoH{} and
back with the two colour LoRAs (deterministic 50-step DDIM, strength 0.20) returns SSIM 0.143 and LAB distance
28.2 against the original crops (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-roundtrip}). Each direction re-encodes through the VAE
(SSIM ceiling 0.58) and redraws the image, so errors likely compound over the round trip. On CAMELYON17~\cite{bandi2018camelyon17}, measured as the
mean pairwise LAB distance $D$ between five hospital centres (2{,}103 patches, $D_{\mathrm{pre}}=56.70$), no
SD\,1.5 configuration brings the centres closer. With fusion the final pipeline is neutral ($\Delta D<0$ would mean closer centres;
$\Delta D=+0.14$, 95\% bootstrap CI $[-0.22, 0.51]$, 4 of 10 centre pairs improved) and worse without it
($+1.29$ $[0.24, 2.49]$, 3 of 10), as is the LCM ladder setting ($+0.83$ $[0.44, 1.19]$, 1 of 10). The CIs
resample the 10 centre pairs, which share centres, so they are supportive only. Likely, the model learns one fixed
Aperio-to-Hamamatsu shift, which moves every centre together rather than towards each other, while the
centres differ by staining protocol as well as scanner, and by more than the gap it was trained on.

\subsection{Downstream clinical utility}
The planned clinical test, an atypia classifier trained on Aperio and scored on normalised Hamamatsu
frames, produced no valid evidence in either direction (Appendix~\ref{app:clinical}). An early result
favouring diffusion had been scored in the wrong direction and is retracted. In the
corrected direction no configuration improves the classifier: our fused pipeline leaves its accuracy
exactly unchanged (difference 0.0, 95\% CI $\pm0.025$, at most about 3 of 120 frames either way). The test
could not have shown a benefit, because the classifier predicts the same grade for about 91\% of frames
whatever the input. Two rebuilt classifiers, a frozen UNI2-h probe and a criteria-supervised model, then
failed their own pre-specified validity gates, so no atypia number is used as evidence.

\subsection{Few-step inference}
Generic LCM-LoRA on the source-conditioned model is a speed--quality trade-off rather than a match: its best
setting (6 nominal steps at strength 0.70, so 4 run, the H4 test; guidance 2.0; tuned on A06/A08, which favours LCM) gives A06 SSIM 0.334 and $\dlab=+9.7$, against 0.373 and
$+21.7$ for 50-step DDIM inversion, and A08 0.515/$+3.0$ against 0.537/$+7.2$. It is 12.4$\times$ faster
(0.50\,s against 6.27\,s per crop, Table~\ref{tab:app-speed}). The likely limit is the sampler, not the step
count: our LCM sampler re-noises at every step, so it does not use the DDIM inversion that gave our largest gain. LCM with inversion was not tested. Distilling a task-specific
LCM from the source-conditioned teacher was worse than the generic adapter under a pre-committed rule.

\subsection{Portability to SDXL}
SDXL transfers the method only with source conditioning and more data than the few-shot budget
(Table~\ref{tab:sdxl}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sdxl}). The ladder configuration (A4) and its warm-started variant (A5) move colour away from
the target, despite SDXL's higher SSIM. Source conditioning works at 512\,px, but natively at 1024\,px the
50-pair model is data-starved: it recovers less colour than doing nothing on every slide. With 96 pairs
recovery turns positive (pooled LAB 39.43 to 32.51), but 144 overlapping crops of the same frames add only
0.11 more, so extra crops of one slide pair do not close the gap. A source ablation on
the 144-pair model (SSIM 0.318 correct, 0.114 shuffled, 0.109 zero) confirms that it uses the source. F3
fusion transfers unchanged (SSIM 0.732), and with the 144-pair model SDXL passes the CAMELYON17 test with a
small effect ($\Delta D=-2.35$ $[-2.97, -1.57]$,
9 of 10 centre pairs closer; $-0.67$ $[-0.96, -0.34]$, 8 of 10, with F3 fusion). In
the \HtoA{} direction, where SD\,1.5 fails ($\dlab=-15.6$), SDXL improves LAB colour at both resolutions, at SSIM 0.36--0.39.

\subsection{Newer backbones (exploratory probes)}
Both newer backbones are feasible to adapt but not yet useful (Table~\ref{tab:cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:results}). An SD\,3.5-large
rank-8 LoRA trains in about five times SD\,1.5's time and fits a 24\,GB GPU. Its colour test is weaker than
planned: a frame-ordering slip drew all 20 crops from the outlier A06, and it ran at 1024\,px without the T5
text encoder, with a bf16 VAE and untuned guidance. On A06 it recovers $\dlab=+4.77$ at SSIM 0.183, more
colour than any SD\,1.5 ladder rung there (at most $+2.38$) at the SSIM of the weakest rungs (0.19), so it is a
feasibility signal, not a general result.
PixCell is the cheapest backbone to adapt and its 16-channel VAE has the highest reconstruction ceiling
(0.773, Fig.~\ref{fig:vaebb}). But generation from a crop's own UNI2-h embedding, without fine-tuning,
scores SSIM 0.045 against the registered target and 0.036 against its own source: a single global embedding
carries no information about where nuclei are (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-pixcell}).
