\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Classical normalisation.} Reinhard et al.~\cite{reinhard2001colortransfer} match per-channel
statistics in a decorrelated colour space; Macenko et al.~\cite{macenko2009normalisation} estimate stain
vectors in optical-density space and rescale stain concentrations. Both need one reference image and no
training, and both rely on assumptions that non-linear scanner differences can break. Using several
reference images improves robustness to varied staining~\cite{ivanov2024multitarget}.

\textbf{Learned normalisation.} StainGAN~\cite{shaban2019staingan} casts normalisation as unpaired
CycleGAN translation~\cite{zhu2017cyclegan}; GAN-based methods transfer colour well but can change
structure~\cite{breen2024gans}. StainNet~\cite{kang2021stainnet} distils a normaliser into a tiny
per-pixel network, and ParamNet~\cite{kang2024paramnet} predicts the weights of a per-image 1$\times$1
colour-mapping network from a low-resolution view. On MITOS-ATYPIA-14, a recent attention-guided residual
method reports high structural similarity with the full paired training set~\cite{madusanka2025attentionresidual}.

\textbf{Diffusion and adapters.} Denoising diffusion~\cite{ho2020ddpm} and its latent variant~\cite{rombach2022ldm}
generate images by reversing a noise process in the latent space of a VAE; DDIM~\cite{song2021ddim} makes
the sampler deterministic and invertible. LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} adds trainable low-rank updates
$h=W_0x+BAx$ to frozen layers, ControlNet~\cite{zhang2023controlnet} injects a spatial condition through a
trainable copy of the encoder, and latent consistency models~\cite{luo2023lcm,luo2023lcmlora} distil
sampling into a few steps. These pieces are mature for natural images; their behaviour on paired scanner
data with a tiny training set is what this work measures.
