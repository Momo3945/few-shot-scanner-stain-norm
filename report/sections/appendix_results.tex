\subsection{Per-slide results}
\label{app:perslide}
Table~\ref{tab:app-perslide} breaks Table~\ref{tab:main} down by slide. Two patterns are hidden by the pooled numbers.
First, the classical methods are unstable from slide to slide: on the four typical slides their colour recovery has a
standard deviation of 5.0--7.0 LAB, against 0.9--2.8 for every trained method, and each is worse than doing nothing
on at least one typical slide (Macenko on A13, Reinhard on A13 and A16, histogram matching on A09 and A16).
Inversion, fusion, ParamNet and StainGAN improve all four. Second, both fused pipelines, SD\,1.5 and SDXL, beat every
classical method on SSIM on every slide, not only pooled. Fusion also leaves PSNR equal to the raw scan's (19.24\,dB)
and lowers MAE slightly (22.41 against 22.97). Pixelwise $\Delta E_{2000}$ is not reported as a separate colour
metric because it tracks SSIM almost rank for rank (Spearman $-0.94$ across the 14 methods of Table~\ref{tab:main}).
For the source-conditioned img2img row, a strength sweep on A06 and A08 (0.20--0.70) traced a smooth
structure--colour trade-off in which no setting beat 0.50 on both.

\subsection{Downstream clinical utility (atypia classifier)}
\label{app:clinical}

\textbf{Protocol.} The proposal's clinical-utility test trains a classifier on raw Aperio $\times$20 frames
of the eleven MITOS-ATYPIA-14 training slides to predict the adjudicated nuclear-atypia score (1--3), then
evaluates it on held-out Hamamatsu frames before and after normalisation. The recovery is the accuracy on
normalised frames minus the accuracy on raw Hamamatsu frames, so a positive value means normalisation
helped the classifier.

\textbf{A retracted result.} The first classifier (a ResNet18, \texttt{atypia\_r18}) appeared to favour
diffusion: the A3 rung at strength 0.50 gave a recovery of $+0.0625$, against $+0.0457$ for the best
classical method, Reinhard. A later audit found that every method had been run \AtoH{}, producing
Hamamatsu-styled images, whereas an Aperio-trained classifier needs \HtoA{} outputs. An A0 output was twice
as close in pixel error to its Aperio source as to its Hamamatsu reference, which confirmed the direction
error. The claim was retracted. Re-running in the correct direction, no configuration beats the raw input:
our source-conditioned \HtoA{} model gives $-0.025$ (CI crossing zero, so no measurable effect), and with F3 fusion the recovery is
exactly $0.0$ (95\% CI $[-0.025,+0.025]$, 120 frames), even though fusion raises its SSIM from 0.548 to
0.730: better structure did not translate into classifier accuracy. The rerun also found and fixed a second bug, in which the raw-Hamamatsu reference silently read
Aperio pixels.

\textbf{The classifier itself is not valid.} \texttt{atypia\_r18} predicts score 2 for about 91\% of held-out
frames even on raw Aperio, its own domain, where it scores 0.55 against 0.50 for always answering 2. Its
0.947 validation accuracy came from two slides containing 46 score-2 frames out of 47, so it is the
majority rate rather than skill. The labels are close to slide-determined: 19 of the 23 score-1 training
frames come from slide A12, and all 38 held-out score-1 frames come from A13 and A16. A retrained variant
with augmentation and class-balanced sampling was worse (raw-Hamamatsu accuracy 0.442 to 0.192).

\textbf{Two rebuilt classifiers, both gated.} Both use frozen UNI2-h features, nested leave-one-slide-out
cross-validation on the 297 training frames, and five pre-specified gates: balanced accuracy $>0.45$,
quadratic weighted kappa (QWK, agreement with the ordered scores beyond chance: 0 is chance, 1 perfect) $>0.2$, no class predicted for more than 80\% of frames, a slide-bootstrap
lower bound on balanced accuracy above chance ($1/3$), and nonzero recall for score 1 on the held-out-in-CV
slide A12. Table~\ref{tab:atypia} gives the results. The linear probe is at chance (balanced accuracy 0.312 against $1/3$). Of three v2 candidates
(an ordinal head, attention-based multiple-instance pooling, and a two-stage model that first predicts the
x40 atypia criteria), the criteria model clears four of five gates but fails the bootstrap lower bound:
its interval $[0.26, 0.73]$ cannot be separated from chance with only eleven slides, and the threshold was
not relaxed afterwards. A separate within-slide test does pass. With each slide's mean removed from both
predictions and labels, so that recognising the slide cannot help, the predicted criteria correlate with
the rated ones (mean Spearman 0.240, CI $[0.148, 0.318]$; nuclei size 0.378, anisonucleosis 0.299,
nucleoli size 0.408, nuclei contour $-0.125$). The correlation is weak, but its interval excludes zero, so frozen features carry real atypia signal, but not
yet enough for a valid score classifier. By protocol the held-out slides were never scored with these
models.

\subsection{Other probes}
\textbf{Differential diffusion.} To protect structure inside the diffusion process, each pixel was given
its own denoising strength from a Canny-derived change map, low near edges and high elsewhere. Six
configurations (dilation radius $\in\{2,4,8\}$, maximum strength $\in\{0.50,0.70\}$) gave SSIM 0.162--0.167
on 20 internal crops (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-diffdiff}). The plain model was scored on only 4 of these crops
($0.162\pm0.006$ over three seeds), where differential diffusion reached 0.184--0.195, so without a matched
20-crop baseline the result is inconclusive rather than negative. It was not pursued: its output is still
decoded by the VAE, so it cannot pass the reconstruction ceiling, which is what motivated fusion outside the U-Net.

\textbf{Round-trip consistency.} Translating A06 crops \AtoH{} and back to Aperio (50-step DDIM, strength
0.20) gives SSIM 0.143, PSNR 13.1\,dB and LAB distance 28.2 against the original
over 64 crops. A faithful round trip would give SSIM 1 and distance 0, and 28.2 exceeds the typical scanner
gap of 25, so the return trip changes colour more than a scanner does (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-roundtrip}).

\subsection{HoVer-Net boundary conditioning}
\label{app:hovernet}
The proposal planned HoVer-Net nuclear-boundary maps as the final structural signal, with Canny edges as the
prototype. We retrained the source-conditioned model with the Canny half of its six-channel input replaced by
1\,px HoVer-Net outlines (\texttt{hovernet\_fast-pannuke}, run on the source crop only), kept every other
setting of the final recipe, and ran the same inversion, fusion and scoring on the 496 held-out crops.
Applied directly to the $\times$20 crops, the PanNuke model, trained at $\times$40, missed almost every
nucleus on Aperio: it found 17.5 nuclei per training crop against 112 on the Hamamatsu scan of the same
tissue, and 0.6 per crop on A06, where 40 of 64 crops had none. Upsampling each crop $2\times$ before
segmentation raised this to 76 per training crop and 19.7 on A06, with no empty crops. The outlines then lie
on nuclei but still miss many pale ones, so the comparison is between sparse nuclear outlines and dense Canny
edges. This segmentation failure is itself an example of the scanner shift this work addresses.

Table~\ref{tab:app-hovernet} gives the result. After fusion, structure is unchanged; before it, pooled SSIM is
slightly lower (lower on A08, A13 and A16, higher on A06 and A09). Windowed LAB is worse on every slide, both before and after fusion.
The loss, about 1.3, is small next to the 25-unit scanner gap, but every interval excludes zero.
The pre-stated criterion for calling HoVer-Net better, a fused SSIM gain whose 95\% CI excludes zero with
windowed LAB no worse, is not met, so the Canny model remains the final pipeline. One possible reason, not
tested here, is that dense Canny edges follow stain intensity and so carry a colour cue that sparse outlines
do not. Each structure was trained once, with one seed.

\subsection{Fine-tuned decoder on unseen centres}
\label{app:decoder-cam}
To test whether the fine-tuned decoder has collapsed onto its training data, we self-reconstructed all 2{,}103
CAMELYON17 patches (five centres, none used in training) with each decoder. The pre-stated condition was that mean
SSIM may not fall more than 0.01, and PSNR not more than 0.5\,dB, below \texttt{ft-mse}. SSIM passes: 0.884 against
0.876 (stock 0.856), higher at every centre (+0.002 to +0.011). PSNR fails: $-1.50$\,dB pooled, about 40\% more squared pixel error (95\% CI $[-1.64,
-1.37]$), from $+0.2$ to $-0.9$\,dB at four centres and $-5.2$\,dB at centre~4, whose patches \texttt{ft-mse}
reconstructs almost exactly (40.1\,dB). LAB distance to the input rises by about 70\%, from 2.61 to 4.49. The fine-tuned decoder
keeps structure on unseen centres but adds a colour shift there, as on MITOS. The final pipeline does not use it.

\subsection{Full-VAE fine-tune}
\label{app:fullvae}
The decoder fine-tune leaves the encoder frozen. As a second step we also trained the encoder, starting from the
fine-tuned decoder and updating all 83.7M VAE parameters with the same data, loss and schedule, plus an anchor
$\lambda\,\lVert E(x)-E_0(x)\rVert^2$ to the original encoder $E_0$. The anchor keeps the latents close enough for
the trained colour LoRA and ControlNet to be reused unchanged. Both $\lambda=1$ and $\lambda=10$ were run, with
checkpoints selected on the internal validation slides. In both arms the latents moved by 0.8--0.9\% of their
variance (cosine similarity 0.996 to the original), so the latent space is practically unchanged.

Table~\ref{tab:app-fullvae} gives the result. Over the decoder fine-tune, self-reconstruction SSIM rises by only
0.002--0.004 on Aperio and 0.001 on Hamamatsu, far below the pre-stated bar of 0.02. Translated SSIM falls
slightly. After fusion, SSIM is unchanged and windowed LAB is 0.4--0.9 worse than the final pipeline. The
$\lambda=10$ arm improves unfused windowed LAB by 1.3 over the decoder fine-tune, on every slide, but the gain does
not survive fusion. The encoder is therefore not a bottleneck worth unfreezing, consistent with the 16-crop
decoder control (Section~\ref{sec:vae}). An unanchored fine-tune, with the LoRA and ControlNet retrained on the new
latents, was not run.

\subsection{SDXL diagnostics and the decision gate}
\label{app:sdxl-diag}
\textbf{Decision gate.} At the end of the ablation the LCM rung (A4) was chosen as the base for SDXL,
and the histopathology-LoRA rung (A5) was also transferred because it beat A4 on slide A06 by 2.6--8.7 in LAB distance, far above
the 0.1--0.3 run-to-run noise floor.

\textbf{Why native-1024 SDXL fails at 50 pairs.} Five diagnostics were run on the 50-pair 1024\,px model.
(D1) A baseline mismatch was ruled out: re-measured against a do-nothing baseline computed natively at
1024\,px, the model still gives $\dlab=-5.25$. (D2) Removing the LoRA makes colour worse ($-8.21$ against $-5.25$), so the LoRA helps by about 3
LAB but is too weak. (D3) RGB-only conditioning matches RGB plus Canny, and Canny alone collapses (SSIM 0.16,
$\dlab=-17.9$), so the edge channels are inert. (D4) No ControlNet scale gives positive recovery, so conditioning strength is not the problem. (D5)
Raising the LoRA scale from 1.0 to 2.0 makes recovery monotonically worse ($-4.38$ to $-8.00$), so the adapter cannot simply be amplified. The model is
data-starved, which the 96- and 144-pair runs confirm.

\textbf{The \AtoH{}/\HtoA{} asymmetry.} SD\,1.5 fails \HtoA{} while SDXL improves its colour. Two diagnostics compared
the mean LAB of model outputs with the real scanner means, which are 17.97 apart. Zero-source ControlNet
outputs could not test the idea that the asymmetry follows the backbone's default colour: every one leaned
towards its own source, because img2img still starts from the source latent. Pure text-to-image samples (Fig.~\ref{fig:app-t2i}) gave partial support (five of
six comparisons consistent): SD\,1.5 leans towards Hamamatsu with either direction's LoRA, which explains
its \HtoA{} failure, and SDXL at 1024\,px \HtoA{} remains an open counterexample: its default colour
leans the wrong way, yet it recovers $\dlab=+9.20$.
