\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 model adapted from 50 coordinate-corresponding pairs can normalise between
two scanners, but only after replacing the planned recipe: a target-only colour LoRA with ControlNet and
LCM barely moves colour, while source conditioning, DDIM inversion and fusion of the learned colour change
onto the source pixels reach SSIM 0.729 and beat the classical baselines on typical-slide colour. The VAE
bottleneck explains most of the gap to the raw scan, and fine-tuning its decoder raises SSIM mainly by
smoothing. Learned nondiffusion baselines trained on the same 50 pairs remain better on colour and SSIM;
ParamNet and the fused pipeline both pass the Relative Dice test (1.003 and 0.994), and the unfused
diffusion output does not. Few-step LCM is 12.5$\times$ faster but does not hold 50-step quality. For
few-shot scanner normalisation, a well-chosen colour map currently beats a resynthesising generative
model, and diffusion is most useful as a source of colour change that fusion projects back onto the
original image. A histopathology-pretrained backbone such as PixCell, given a source-image conditioning
branch, is the natural next step.
