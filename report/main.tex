\begin{abstract}
Scanner-specific colour shifts degrade histopathology models when they are deployed on new hardware.
We ask whether a frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 model, adapted with LoRA, ControlNet and LCM-LoRA from at
most 50 coordinate-corresponding Aperio/Hamamatsu crop pairs, can normalise between the two scanners of
MITOS-ATYPIA-14 while preserving tissue structure. A six-rung ablation shows that the target-domain
colour adapter recovers little colour and that ControlNet mainly improves structure. Conditioning the
adapter on the source image, inverting the source latent with DDIM, and fusing the learned colour change
back onto the untouched source pixels give the best diffusion result within the 50-pair budget: SSIM 0.729 against
the real Hamamatsu scan, close to the raw scan's 0.732 and above every classical baseline, and windowed colour closer to the target than Macenko,
Reinhard or histogram matching on typical slides. We also show that the VAE caps every unfused diffusion output
and that a fine-tuned decoder raises SSIM mainly by smoothing. Two learned nondiffusion baselines
trained on the same 50 pairs, ParamNet and StainGAN, outperform every diffusion configuration on colour
and SSIM. On the Relative Dice structure-safety gate, ParamNet (1.003) and our fused pipeline (0.994)
pass, while the unfused diffusion outputs do not. No SD\,1.5 configuration brings the CAMELYON17 centres
closer together, and few-step LCM inference does not match 50-step DDIM quality.
\end{abstract}
